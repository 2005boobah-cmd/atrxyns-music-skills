# Genre and craft reference

Use starting points rather than rigid definitions. Adapt to subgenre, era, song, and chosen references. Give hybrid genres distinct musical roles.

| Genre family | Prioritize | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rap / hip-hop | Groove, stress placement, internal and multisyllabic rhyme, phrasing across bars, delivery, breath, rhetorical or narrative movement | Equal couplets throughout; arbitrary homophones; speed replacing substance; prose without rhythmic design |
| Pop | Recognizable phrase, rhythmic/melodic motif, emotional focus, section contrast | Crowding sustained notes; generic declarations; constant peak intensity |
| R&B / soul | Pocket, expressive phrasing, melodic space, intimacy, room for harmonies and runs | Filling every subdivision; demanding runs on every line; vague sensual imagery |
| Rock / alternative / punk | Strong stresses, vocal rhythm against riffs, physical energy, distinct refrains, subgenre attitude | Forcing rock into industrial/electronic production; unsingable ornamental language |
| Metal / heavy rock | Riff-aware phrasing, rhythmic attack, vocal contrasts, dynamic tension, subgenre instruments | Equating heaviness with techno; consonant congestion; constant intensity |
| Country / folk / Americana | Scene, character, believable details, conversational phrasing, developing refrain, consequence | Stock trucks/whiskey props; forced dialect; explaining every image |
| Singer-songwriter / acoustic | Intimate perspective, specific images, understated turns, melody-friendly phrasing, silence | Abstract emotion lists; excessively neat resolutions |
| Jazz / standards / swing | Phrase shape across harmony, economy, elasticity, internal sound, interpretation | Equating swing with archaic slang; dense syllables on every note |
| Electronic / dance / house / trance | Short vocal motifs, repetition/variation, tension/release, build/drop roles, instrumental space | Overwritten narrative where a motif serves better; treating lyrics as the whole arrangement |
| Funk / disco | Syncopation, rhythmic hooks, bass/drum interaction, call and response | Continuous syllables obscuring groove; excess directions in lyrics |
| Reggae / ska / related styles | Subgenre-specific rhythm, clarity, melodic/rhythmic refrains | Fabricated patois; reducing genre to a stereotyped accent |
| Musical theatre / narrative | Character objective, playable action, changing stakes, distinct voices | Exposition without action; every character sounding alike |
| Ambient / experimental / instrumental | Timbre, texture, space, development, recurrence/transformation, optional sparse vocals | Forcing lyrics or conventional choruses into instrumental briefs |
| Other traditions | Research structure, language, rhythm, instruments, performance practice | Treating this table as exhaustive; inventing cultural conventions |

## Revision moves

- Reveal emotion through an action, object, or exchange.
- Shorten language around musical focal points; let important words register.
- Preserve speech stress instead of demanding mispronunciation.
- Vary entry points, phrase length, rhyme placement, and articulation; speed is one option.
- Revisit an image with a changed implication rather than stacking unrelated metaphors.
- Cut impressive lines that break character, context, melody, or progression.
- Reject double meanings whose second reading needs an external explanation.
- Do not assume more rhyme, sections, explicitness, or complexity improves every song.

## Research grounding

Use principles, not borrowed lyrics. Reopen sources when citations or close analysis are requested. Do not claim these sources certify a generated song.

- Kyle Adams, *On the Metrical Techniques of Flow in Rap Music*, Music Theory Online 15.5 (2009): https://mtosmt.org/classic/mto.09.15.5/mto.09.15.5.adams.html . Consider accent/rhyme placement, syntax/bar relationships, and articulation.
- John J. Mattessich, *This Flow Ain't Free*, Music Theory Online 25.1 (2019): https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.19.25.1/mto.19.25.1.mattessich.php . Consider alignment/tension between vocal rhythm and instrumental track.
- Berklee Online, *Lyric Writing: Writing Lyrics to Music*: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/lyric-writing-writing-lyrics-to-music . Check natural stress, phrasing, and focal-point placement.
- Berklee Online, *Lyric Writing: Tools and Strategies*: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/lyric-writing-tools-and-strategies . Consider rhyme, line length, movement, contrast, and emotional intent.
- Berklee Online, *Lyric Writing: Writing From the Title*: https://online.berklee.edu/courses/lyric-writing-writing-from-the-title . Develop the central phrase's implications across sections.

Treat the table and checklist as practical synthesis, not individual findings established by these sources.
