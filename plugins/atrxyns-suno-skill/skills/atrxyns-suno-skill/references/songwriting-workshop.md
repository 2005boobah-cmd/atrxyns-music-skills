# Songwriting workshop

Use judgment, not a checklist of mandatory tricks. Apply only the techniques that serve the brief. Improve the writing rather than making every song longer or more technical.

## Hook laboratory

Identify the hook's job: memorable phrase, emotional question, rhythmic motif, communal chant, character statement, or instrumental refrain. Briefly try two distinct approaches when none is provided. Prefer a phrase whose meaning and sounds are clear on first hearing; let deeper context reward later listens.

Check:
- Can the central phrase be recalled after one reading, without the setup paragraph?
- Do the important vowels and stresses leave room for a sung phrase or rhythmic anchor?
- Is there a recognizably different rhythm or density from the verse?
- Does repetition create anticipation rather than merely occupy characters?
- Does the hook express this particular song, or could it fit fifty unrelated songs unchanged?

Do not enforce title-equals-hook in genres where another form works. Do not force a sung hook onto a requested continuous rap verse, ambient piece, or through-composed story.

## Section architecture and dramatic movement

For each section, identify one new contribution. A verse can add an incident or consequence; a pre-chorus can narrow toward a decision or raise tension; a bridge can change perspective, strip away an excuse, or introduce a contrasting musical space. These are options, not required roles.

Do not confuse more sadness, louder delivery, or longer words with development. Avoid the automatic ending where the narrator suddenly learns a life lesson. Finish with a credible action, unresolved implication, image, musical release, joke, or return suited to the brief.

For fiction or a staged narrator, maintain timeline and character knowledge. For personal writing, use only facts supplied or confirmed by the user. Do not invent a child, job, addiction, diagnosis, relationship event, or income detail to make a song feel intimate.

## Performance and prosody

Sketch a plausible performance rather than treating line breaks as bars. Place natural stressed syllables where the musical emphasis is likely to fall. Check breath-length phrases, repeated consonants, and words stretched over notes. Prefer open vowels on long exposed notes when they fit the voice and meaning; do not rewrite every lyric for an imagined belter.

For proposed rap patterns, vary entry points, rests, phrase length, rhyme location, and articulation. Let some syntax run across the bar and let other thoughts stop sharply. Use double-time selectively; write meaningful connected phrases rather than equal-length syllable strings. A technical burst should have a comprehensible start, turn, and landing.

For melodic genres, contrast verse and chorus using phrase length, repetition, note space, register direction, or arrangement. Describe register and contour as intended direction unless actual notes have been composed. Do not claim a written lyric has a verified melody.

For duration estimates, use the requested groove, plausible phrase length, number of repeated sections, and instrumental room. Label the result a target. Sparse ballads and dense rap cannot share one words-per-minute rule.

## Rhyme and wordplay audit

Build around pronunciation and stress, not spelling. Use connected rhyme families when helpful, then move or release them before predictability takes over. Permit slant rhyme, unrhymed lines, and simple end rhymes when they suit the song.

For an intended double meaning:
1. State both readings briefly in working notes.
2. Check that the setup supports both; a shared vocabulary field alone is not a second meaning.
3. Check grammar, target-accent pronunciation, and an ordinary first-listen reading.
4. Check that the bar advances character, thought, humor, conflict, or musical energy.
5. Remove the trick if it needs an explanatory lyric afterward.

Distinguish homophones from repeated words used in different senses and from near-homophones. Do not claim a triple entendre merely because three related nouns appear. Do not impose a quota of puns. Avoid familiar pairs such as peace/piece, sole/soul, and prophet/profit unless the setup earns a fresh use. A quieter original observation can outperform an obvious pun.

Check rhyme chains without their rhyming endings. If the middle is vague, repetitive, or logically broken, rewrite the thought before improving the sound. Never sacrifice natural syntax just to claim more internal rhymes.

## Specificity and freshness

Use actions and details that reveal a situation; do not merely swap nouns into stock metaphors. Permit common language when it sounds true. Avoid treating an absolute banned-word list as creativity.

Before finalizing, compare against the recent visible song history. Watch for repeated scaffolding: financial struggle as a default opening, a phone as the universal symbol of alienation, a child providing the revelation, or a final verse about finishing the song itself. These devices remain available when requested or developed freshly; do not use them automatically to simulate depth.

Limit competing metaphor systems. Keep an extended image coherent, then return to ordinary speech if that is clearer. Do not mechanically make each line a quotable aphorism. Allow an unadorned sentence to carry the emotional turn.

For a revision of an existing song, freshness does not mean destroying its identity. Preserve established imagery, perspective, and hook when the user likes them.

## Styles and arrangement

Lead with the core genre and rhythmic identity, then the voice, defining instruments, and essential arrangement/dynamic instructions. Describe the desired result positively. Add exclusions only for real unwanted tendencies; long negative lists can fight the positive brief.

Check contradictions explicitly: clean intimate vocal versus constant growling; sparse acoustic verse versus nonstop stacked synths; organic soul versus a blanket exclusion of all rough vocal texture. Some contrasts are intentional arrangements: make the section distinction clear.

Separate gritty vocal texture from distorted mixing, and soulful intensity from yelling. Distinguish swung/triplet feel from a constant triplet rap cadence. Express preferences precisely enough that desired traits do not get excluded accidentally.

Do not embed unsupported control names in settings. Treat tempo, key, arrangement, mic distance, and timbre as text direction when no corresponding verified form field exists. Minimize section tags; do not turn the lyric field into a production essay.

## Revision from feedback

Use the narrowest change that addresses the actual problem:

| Reported problem | First adjustment | Preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Too dark, grungy, or industrial | Rewrite the positive sound brief around cleaner timbre, groove, and instrumentation; remove conflicting gritty directions | Lyrics and successful hooks unless implicated |
| Flat or repetitive flow | Vary phrasing, stress, rests, rhyme landing points, and section density | Subject and meaningful bars |
| Clever but meaningless | Repair the thought/setup and remove weak puns | Earned wordplay |
| Chorus does not stick | Simplify the central phrase and sharpen rhythmic/melodic contrast | Best hook idea or user-favored phrase |
| Words are swallowed | Shorten congested phrases and simplify consonant clusters; request clearer delivery | Important images and natural syntax |
| Too many runs or ad-libs | Specify restrained ornamentation with room around the lead | Soulfulness and desired emotion |
| Song rambles or feels too long | Remove duplicate section jobs and surplus repeated material | Narrative turn and musical release |
| Wrong voice or too much processing | Adjust voice direction, relevant selector, and targeted exclusions | Arrangement and core song identity |
| Style prompt ignored | Simplify priority order, resolve conflicts, then adjust available style controls deliberately | Core genre and reference intent |

When audio is available and supported, cite actual moments heard. Otherwise ground diagnosis in the user's description and label uncertain causes as hypotheses. Prefer one focused revision rather than changing lyrics, voice, genre, and every slider at once. Do not require the user to purchase a model or spend extra credits to test an idea.

## Final critical pass

Find the weakest section or few lines and revise those. Confirm that the song says what the user wanted, has a deliberate musical identity, and can plausibly be delivered by the intended voice. Do not output artificial scores, guarantee hits, or present text checks as proof that the song has been rendered successfully.
