# Understand the song before choosing pictures

The user requires deeply thought-out lyric interpretation. Complete this creative analysis before sourcing media; a generic mood, title keyword or genre label is insufficient. Keep the analysis practical and save it with the video project.

1. Read the full lyrics twice: first for the complete story, then for details that change its meaning. State the narrator's situation, outward desire, underlying need, central conflict and final realization. Distinguish explicit lyrics from interpretation; do not invent biography.
2. Map every section, including spoken lines, pre-choruses, bridges and outro. For each record: exact lyric anchor, what it means in context, emotional state, change from the previous section, and its role in the whole song. Read ambiguous lines together with surrounding lines, rather than illustrating isolated words.
3. Track repeated images and phrases. Explain how each chorus means something different after the intervening verses. Identify contradictions, subtext, perspective shifts, emotional turning points and the most consequential quiet lines. Let these control the visual arc.
4. Write a visual thesis: how the real imagery will communicate the song's specific conflict and resolution. Build connected locations, motifs and changes in framing/light. Balance literal details with grounded visual metaphor; avoid random pretty scenery, stock clichés, one illustrative shot per noun, and an unchanged sunset montage.
5. Create a section-to-shot table before downloading: lyric anchor, meaning, real photograph/clip brief, reason for that image, transition and recurring motif. Every shot must earn its place. When a line mentions family or a child, do not present stock actors as the artist's actual family; use sensitive anonymous details or environmental imagery when appropriate.
6. Compare this plan to the actual recording. Refine section boundaries, pacing, holds and transitions around vocal delivery, musical shifts, silence and emotional emphasis. A technical rap verse and an intimate pause need different pacing even when the color palette stays connected.
7. Review the completed sequence against the whole lyric: Does it establish the conflict, make the turning point perceptible, develop repeated choruses, and make the ending feel earned? Remove shots that could accompany almost any song. Keep interpretations restrained where the words are uncertain.

## Review what the actual shot communicates

For each candidate, record: the exact lyrical or musical beat, the intended viewer understanding or feeling, the visible evidence in the clip that supports it, and how it works with the preceding and following shots. Inspect the footage, not just its search title. Reject a candidate if its rationale depends on imagining behavior absent from the frame.

Ask whether the same shot and explanation could fit many unrelated songs. If so, find a more specific action, detail, environment or consequence. Generic chatting is not a visual shorthand for every social conflict; generic phone use is not automatically an apology, and a smile is not automatically reconciliation. People are welcome when their visible action, expression, setting and framing support the intended beat. Do not turn this preference into a ban on people or require literal reenactment of every lyric.

Example: in a barbershop rumor story, haircut footage concretely establishes the setting and a mirror can support self-examination. An unrelated friendly conversation does not show a rumor spreading or its consequences merely because people are speaking. A phone shot should earn its place through the communication beat and sequence, without inventing readable messages. Do not add theft imagery when the story says the accused person did not steal.

Before the full render, review the selected clips in sequence against the intended emotional progression. Replace shots that are merely on-topic, contradict the story, imply an unearned resolution or require captions to explain away their mismatch. A detailed scene plan cannot rescue an unsuitable clip.

Use only real photographs and real video clips under the main skill's sourcing rule. Analysis is not a substitute for an actual finished video. Share a brief creative rationale with the user, then continue production without introducing an approval gate.
